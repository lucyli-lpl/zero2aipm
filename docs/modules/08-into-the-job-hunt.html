Module 08

Into the Job Hunt

Turn the evidence from Modules 3 to 7 into a resume and interview answers that show you can ship AI products.

Time
1 to 2 weeks, then per interview
You leave with
A resume draft, a self-introduction and three practiced stories
Do this module with your AI tutor: open the repository in your coding agent and type start the course or continue.

Why this module

By now you have shipped products, written decision records, analyzed failures, torn down trends and briefed yourself on industries. That is evidence. This module turns it into a resume and interviews.

Resume principles for AI PM and AI product operations roles

  • One storyline. Find what all your experiences have in common and lead with it. Mine was turning complex human judgment into rules AI can execute and verify, then getting people to use them.
  • A separate AI capabilities section, near the top, with evidence on every line. Group by capability (boundaries, context, agents, evals, cost and latency, prototyping), not by tool names.
  • Every AI bullet shows an AI-specific action: defining boundaries, building an eval set, analyzing bad cases, designing fallbacks, choosing a model for cost and latency.
  • Every number must survive "how did you calculate that?" Leave out what you cannot explain.
  • Personal projects show a decision and its trade-off, not just what you built. Link to the live product.
  • Length follows content. Up to two pages. The first page must stand on its own.
  • Education goes last once you have a few years of experience.

Interview principles

  • Prepare one self-introduction, then prepare for being interrupted. Interviewers cut in at the project they care about. Know each project well enough to pick it up from any point.
  • Leave hooks. State a result without the whole method ("raised accuracy from 82% to 95%", "found a channel nobody in the company had used"). Interviewers ask, and what they draw out is more convincing than what you volunteer.
  • Explain a career switch as moving toward something, not running from your old industry.
  • Explain a gap honestly: what you reflected on, how you refocused, and what you produced afterwards.
  • For "how would you use AI in our business", use your industry brief from Module 6 and the five questions.
  • For "design an agent for X", start from the business goal and the human-in-the-loop points (Module 4), not from the architecture.
  • Research the interviewer. Senior leaders often speak publicly about their AI strategy. Connect your projects to what they have said.
  • Record your interviews (with consent where required) and review them. Your weakest answers will repeat until you fix them.

Exercise

  1. With your tutor, collect your evidence: projects, decision records, eval results, teardowns, industry briefs and published work.
  2. Ask your tutor to research what AI PM or AI product operations job descriptions currently ask for in your market. Your tutor will search and show sources.
  3. Draft your storyline sentence and your AI capabilities section. Every line needs evidence.
  4. Write a two-minute self-introduction. Ask your tutor to interrupt you at the project section, as a real interviewer would.
  5. Pick three stories: one shipped product, one failure you fixed, one industry recommendation. Practice each with your tutor playing a skeptical interviewer.
  6. After each real interview, write down every question and how you answered. Review it with your tutor.

Check yourself

  • Does the top third of your resume tell someone who you are and what you are strongest at?
  • Can you defend every number on it?
  • Have you practiced being interrupted?

Companion skills

Two skills built from this journey will live in the skills/ folder of this repository: one for writing AI PM resumes and one for interview preparation. Check the folder for what is available.

From my journey

I interviewed across several industries in March and April 2026. My first answers about my career gap were defensive and did not work. Rewriting them as "here is what I reflected on and what I built once I refocused" did. The self-introduction I finally used was one version for every interviewer, and the time I saved went into preparing for interruptions. Six weeks after my first weekend of building, I accepted an AI product manager offer.
