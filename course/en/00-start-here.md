# Module 0 · Start Here

## Why this course exists

In March 2026 I was a fintech product manager with six years of experience, a gap on my resume, and a lot of AI articles bookmarked. I had been "following AI" for months. What changed things was not one more article. It was the week I paused my job applications, built three small AI products, and wrote down every day what I did and what broke.

Six weeks later I had an AI product manager offer. This course is the path I took, cleaned up so you can walk it faster.

Two things to know before you start:

- **This course teaches a path and methods, not a fixed body of knowledge.** AI tools and buzzwords change every few months. Anything in these modules that names a specific tool, model or trend carries a date. Treat those as examples of the method, and check what is current when you get there.
- **You learn by doing, with an AI agent as your tutor.** Every module ends with an exercise you do yourself, on your own files. Your tutor explains, asks questions and checks your work. It does not do the exercise for you.

## How the course runs

1. Clone or fork this repository and open it in an AI coding agent that can read and write files, such as Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI or Cursor.
2. Type `start the course`. Your tutor reads the course instructions, asks a few questions about your background, and walks you through one module at a time.
3. Your work goes into the `my-journey/` folder: your learning log, glossary, project notes and decision records. Your tutor keeps track of your progress in `my-journey/progress.md`.
4. You can stop at any point. Next time, type `continue` and your tutor picks up where you left off.

Only have a chat app such as ChatGPT or Gemini? Use the paste-in prompt in `TUTOR_PROMPT.md` and paste one module at a time. It works, but you keep your own files and paste them in when your tutor asks.

## The one habit: a learning log

The single most useful thing I did was keep a daily log in a plain spreadsheet. One row per item, with five columns:

| Date | What I worked on | Status | Notes | Links and takeaways |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 03-13 | Customer service agent on a no-code platform | Done | | Published it. The workflow finally ran end to end. |
| 03-11 | Local RAG with Ollama and Docker | In progress | Network kept dropping | Models pulled, not wired together yet |
| 03-11 | LoRA fine-tuning video | Done | Too costly to try for real | Know what it is and when it is worth it |

Why it works:

- It makes progress visible on days that feel like nothing happened.
- The "Notes" column records why things stalled. Months later, those are the most useful lines, because they show what to skip.
- It becomes raw material for your resume, your interviews and anything you write about your journey.

Keep it boring. A log you update every day beats a beautiful one you abandon.

## Exercise

Work through these with your tutor:

1. Tell your tutor your background in two or three sentences: your current role, what you already know about AI, and why you want to move into AI product work.
2. Your tutor creates `my-journey/learning-log.md` from the template in `templates/`. Add your first entry: what you did today to start this course.
3. Write down one sentence answering: "In six weeks, I want to be able to show ___." Make it something someone else could look at, such as a live product, a written teardown or an interview you passed. Your tutor saves it at the top of your log.

## Check yourself

- Can you explain, in one sentence, why this course carries dates on tools and trends?
- Is your log somewhere you will actually open every day?
- Is your six-week goal something visible to someone other than you?

## From my journey

My first log entry was a free introductory AI course. My second was a note to myself to stop sending five applications a day and build something over the weekend instead. That second entry is the one that mattered.
